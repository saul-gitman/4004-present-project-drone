1 · The question

The fence buffer needs a velocity

Position least squares already gives σh through HDOP. The buffer also grows with speed, because the drone keeps flying for Δt before a new decision bites.

rbuffer = rdrone + k·σh + v·Δt σh = HDOP · σUEREfrom position LS v = ?this deck

k = 3.03 from P(miss) = 10⁻⁴. Δt = 1.0 s GNSS age + 0.1 s compute + 0.5 s reaction = 1.6 s. Values from params.py.

At 10 m/s the speed term (16 m) is as big as the geometry term in open sky, so a wrong or noisy v moves the fence decision as much as a bad HDOP.

2 · One satellite

Doppler measures how fast the range changes

The receiver tracks the carrier frequency shift fD. Times the wavelength, it is the range rate: the relative velocity projected on the line of sight, plus clock drift.

ρ̇i = −λ·fD,iλ = c / fL1 = 0.1903 m ρ̇i = eiT(vsat − vu) + c·δṫ ρ̇i − eiTvsat = −eiTvu + c·δṫknown left, unknown right

The satellite term is hundreds of m/s and comes from the ephemeris. The drone term is a few m/s. One satellite gives one projection of vu, so we need at least four for [vE, vN, vU, c·δṫ].

3 · Stack the satellites

It is the H you already built for position

Each satellite gives a row [−eiT, 1]. The position solve uses those rows for Δρ, and the Doppler solve uses them for z. Line-of-sight vectors and elevation weights are computed once.

Δρ = H·[Δx; Δb]position z = H·[vu; c·δṫ]velocity Q = (HTWH)−1,  wi = sin²eli

Same Q means the same HDOP for both. Street canyon: buildings hide the satellites across the street, the geometry stretches, and position and velocity get worse together.

4 · One least-squares solve

Velocity, with a covariance that tells the truth

No iteration: the Doppler model is already linear. The covariance uses the same Q, scaled by the range-rate noise.

[v̂; c·δṫ̂] = (HTWH)−1HTWz cov = σρ̇²·Q,   σv,h = HDOP·σρ̇

5 · Filter one: residuals

A bad satellite shows up in the residuals

A reflected (NLOS) signal adds a bias the geometry cannot explain. Healthy residuals follow χ² with n − 4 degrees of freedom; a biased one pushes the sum over the threshold.

r = z − H·[v̂; c·δṫ̂] SSE = rTWr / σ²  ~  χ²(n − 4) SSE > T(n−4, 10⁻³) ⇒ drop worst, solve again

"Worst" means the largest standardised residual |ri| / √(W⁻¹ − HQHᵀ)ii. Try G05 or G13: low or lonely satellites need a much bigger bias before the test sees it. The same test runs on the pseudoranges in spp.fde. A position fix is only called verified with at least 3 redundant satellites left. With less, two NLOS signals can hide inside the residuals.

6 · Filter two: an IMU prior

Least squares with a prior is the Kalman filter

Predict v from the last estimate plus IMU acceleration, then add that prediction to the least-squares cost. The minimiser is exactly the Kalman update.

x⁻ = x + aIMUΔt,   P⁻ = P + (σaΔt)²I min ‖z − Hx‖²R⁻¹ + ‖x − x⁻‖²(P⁻)⁻¹R⁻¹ = W/σ² x̂ = (HTR−1H + (P⁻)−1)−1(HTR−1z + (P⁻)−1x⁻)    ≡ x⁻ + K(z − Hx⁻)Kalman gain form

σa is the one knob: small trusts the IMU, large trusts GNSS. Under the overpass only 3 satellites remain, so plain least squares has no answer, but the prior still lets those 3 rows correct v.

7 · Closing the loop

v sets the buffer, and the buffer sets the speed

Every epoch: distance to the fence from the estimated position, buffer from σh and closing speed, then the fastest closing speed that still fits.

rbuffer = rdrone + k·σh + vclose·Δt vcap = (d − rdrone − k·σh) / Δt vclose = max(0, −v̂·n̂)towards the fence only

CLEAR flies as planned, SLOW cuts the closing speed to vcap, STOP hovers. In the 30 m canyon only 5 satellites remain, so the fix cannot be verified and σh falls back to 50 m. The drone stays about 150 m back. Simulation runs at 2× speed.

8 · Summary

One least-squares engine, three safety numbers

Nothing here is outside the syllabus. The step that is new is the last one: turning position, velocity and residuals into a decision the drone acts on.

Future work: on-board sensors. The same buffer logic gives a speed limit for any sensing range Rs: you cannot outrun your headlights, Rs ≥ rdrone + v·Δt + v²/2a.
